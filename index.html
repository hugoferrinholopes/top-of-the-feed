Top of the Feed

A classroom game on social media feeds. GOVT 3421, Youth and Politics.

Your role
Feed editor

You decide what students see first on Campus, a social app used by the students of one city.

Your goal: in each round, put one post at the top of the feed. Every choice trades off attention, accuracy and agreement.

How to play
  1. Read what is happening in the city.
  2. Select one of the four posts for the top of the feed.
  3. Select Reveal to see what your choice does.
  4. After three rounds, choose one rule for the whole feed.
Words you will need
Attention: how long users keep scrolling, reading and commenting.
Accuracy: how far what users see is true and reliable.
Agreement: how much the feed shows users what they already think.
Engagement ranking: a feed that puts first the posts most likely to draw likes, comments and shares.
Newest first: a feed that shows posts in the order they were written, the most recent at the top.

Results are shown as High, Medium or Low. These ranks are illustrative: they show directions, not measurements.

Part 2. One rule for the whole feed

You can no longer pick posts one by one. Choose the rule that ranks every post on Campus, then select Reveal.

Select a rule first.

Your feed

What the game shows. No post and no rule is high on attention and accuracy and low on agreement at once. The posts that hold attention tend to tell users what they already think or to make them angry, and false claims travel with them. Accurate posts, and calm posts from the other side, hold attention less well.
